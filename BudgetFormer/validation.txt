# Validation Protocol and Experimental Results

## 1. Experimental Protocol

### Proof of Concept (POC)
- Standard Multi-Head Attention Baseline
- BudgetFormer (Dynamic Adaptive Budget)

### Ablations
- Fixed Budget Allocation ($s \in \{0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0\}$)
- Random Head Selection (Unlearned Routing)

### Behavioral Analysis
- Active head distribution $k(x)$: Mean predicted budget $s_{\text{mean}}$ and variability across inputs
- Input complexity vs. Budget correlation (Simple inputs vs. Complex inputs, e.g., Entailment vs. Neutral in SNLI)

### Attention Head Visualizations
- Heatmaps of head utilization across layers
- Analysis of head specialization and redundancy

### Robustness and Generalization
- Scaled backbones: BERT, RoBERTa, DistilBERT, DeBERTa, Longformer
- Comparative baselines: Classic Head Pruning (Michel et al., 2019; Voita et al., 2019) and Sparse Attention
- Decoder architectures with causal masking and KV caching

## 3. Implementation and Methodology Details

1. **Architecture Configurations**:
   - Official paper results: 4 layers (blocks), 8 attention heads, $d_{\text{model}} = 768$.
   - Exploratory POC configuration: 6 layers, 12 attention heads, $d_{\text{model}} = 768$.

2. **Entropy Schedule ($\beta_{\text{schedule}}$)**:
   - Implemented as $\beta(t) = \beta_{\max} (1 - \frac{2t}{T})$, decreasing from $+\beta_{\max}$ to $-\beta_{\max}$.
   - Early training phase: $+\beta(t)$ encourages high head selection entropy (exploration across all heads).
   - Late training phase: $-\beta(t)$ penalizes diffuse selections, encouraging peaked head importance and specialization (exploitation).

3. **Theoretical vs. Physical Inference Savings**:
   - Standard PyTorch computes full attention tensors before applying the top-k mask.
   - Reported savings reflect theoretical complexity and sparse execution potential, distinct from dense GPU tensor kernel latency.

4. **Carbon Footprint**:
   - Approximated using theoretical FLOPs, A100 GPU power rating (300W), and a grid emission factor ($0.4 \text{ kg CO}_2/\text{kWh}$).

## 4. Problem Statement and Contributions

Transformers have become the dominant architecture for natural language processing tasks, largely due to the effectiveness of multi-head attention. In standard designs, multiple attention heads are used in parallel under the assumption that each head captures complementary information. However, the actual contribution of multiple heads to model performance remains insufficiently understood.

In this work, we conduct an empirical study across several NLP benchmarks, including AG News, IMDB, SNLI, Yelp Review Full, and DBpedia. We observe that increasing the number of attention heads yields negligible improvements in accuracy, and in some cases, a single head performs comparably or even slightly better. These findings suggest that multi-head attention may be significantly over-parameterized for a wide range of tasks.

Motivated by this observation, we propose an adaptive attention mechanism that dynamically adjusts the number of active heads based on the input. Instead of relying on a fixed and manually tuned number of heads, our approach learns to allocate computational resources where needed. The method introduces a lightweight gating mechanism that controls both the importance and the number of active heads during inference.

Our experiments show that the proposed adaptive model matches or exceeds the performance of standard Transformers while significantly reducing computational cost, including FLOPs and memory usage. Furthermore, unlike fixed sparsity baselines, our approach does not require task-specific tuning and generalizes across datasets.

### Key Contributions:
1. Empirical evidence demonstrating that multi-head attention is consistently over-parameterized across standard benchmarks.
2. A dynamic head selection and budget gating mechanism that adapts computation per input sample.
3. Demonstration of competitive classification performance alongside marked reductions in theoretical FLOPs, memory footprint, and estimated carbon emissions.
